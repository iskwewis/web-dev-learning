Welcome

Welcome to the Indigenous Artists Showcase. This website celebrates the work and creative perspectives of Indigenous artists working with acrylic paint. Explore the artists, learn about their stories and artistic practices, and discover a collection of their artwork..

Art Medium

Acrylic Painting

Acrylic painting is a versatile medium that allows artists to explore colour, storytelling, culture, identity, and personal experiences. Indigenous artists use acrylic paint in many different ways, creating artwork that reflects their individual perspectives and connections to their communities and cultures.


There isn't one single style of Indigenous acrylic painting. Each artist brings their own experiences, techniques, symbols, and visual language to their work. This showcase highlights three Indigenous artists: Jackie Traverse, Kevin Cardinal, and Kalum Teke Dan, and explores how each artist uses acrylic painting to express their unique perspectives.

Featured Artists

Kevin Cardinal

Kevin is a Woodland Cree artist and a member of Bigstone Cree Nation in Treaty 8 Territory. Working primarily with acrylic paint, his artwork explores his connection to Cree culture, family, spirituality, and personal experiences, often using animals to represent people, emotions, and stories.

Jackie Traverse

Jackie Traverse is an Anishinaabe multidisciplinary artist from Winnipeg, Manitoba. Working in acrylic, oil, mixed media, sculpture, and animation, her work draws from her culture, experiences, and perspectives as an Indigenous woman.

Kalum Teke Dan

Kalum Teke Dan is a Blackfoot artist from the Blood Tribe in Southern Alberta. His work includes acrylic, oil, and watercolour painting, with a focus on portraits and wildlife inspired by his culture, family, and observations of the world around him.
