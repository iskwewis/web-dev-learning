About the Artists

Kevin Cardinal

Kevin Cardinal is a Woodland Cree artist from Wabasca, Alberta, and a member of Bigstone Cree Nation in Treaty 8 Territory. He began drawing and sketching at a young age and developed his artistic practice through experimentation with colour and different techniques. Working primarily with acrylic paint, Cardinal's artwork is influenced by his culture, family, beliefs, personal experiences, and connection to the Creator.


Animals are an important part of Cardinal's artwork and can represent people, spirits, emotions, beliefs, and life experiences. His work reflects his connection to Cree culture and community while bringing his own experiences and perspectives into his paintings. Cardinal has also contributed his artwork to community projects, including murals and Indigenous language revitalization initiatives.

Jackie Traverse

Jackie Traverse is an Anishinaabe multidisciplinary artist from Winnipeg, Manitoba, and a member of Lake St. Martin First Nation. She works across several mediums, including acrylic and oil painting, mixed media, sculpture, and animation. Her artistic practice is influenced by her culture, personal experiences, family, and perspectives as an Indigenous woman. She studied Fine Arts at the University of Manitoba and graduated in 2009.


Traverse's work explores themes of family, identity, culture, and social issues affecting Indigenous communities. Her artwork often reflects her experiences and observations while creating space for conversations about Indigenous women and communities. Alongside her visual art, Traverse has also created animation exploring topics such as the Sixties Scoop and Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls.

Kalum Teke Dan

Kalum Teke Dan is a Blackfoot artist from the Blood Tribe in Southern Alberta and works from Calgary. Largely self-taught, he has developed an artistic practice that includes acrylic, oil, and watercolour painting. His grandparents, who were known for their beadwork and traditional regalia, were an early influence on his interest in art and continue to be part of the inspiration behind his creative practice.


Dan is particularly known for his portraits and wildlife paintings. His portraits are inspired by real people and aim to convey strength and pride, while his animal artwork explores the spirit and character of the animals he observes. His cultural background, connection to family, participation in powwows and ceremonies, and relationship with Blackfoot traditions all influence his work.
